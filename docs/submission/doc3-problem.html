The problem: exams that cannot be trusted

Saakshi is a computer exam system where every answer is signed, every failure is survived and every decision can be checked.

NEET-UG 2026: a leak at the source

Insiders at a printing press scanned papers before dispatch. The leak spread to more than 10 states. The CBI made arrests. The leak came from the people who handle the paper, not from the exam hall.

NEET-UG 2024: unfair fixes

1,563 candidates got grace marks for lost time. The marks were scrapped and a re-test was offered. Only 813 took it. The court noted wrong paper sets and weak CCTV.

SSC 2025: the insider at the centre

Exams were cancelled after crashes and biometric failures. At Dhanbad the server manager was arrested. Answers were auto-selected while candidates only moved the mouse.

JEE Main 2025: no proof for the candidate

A pop-up showed 46 questions attempted. The response sheet showed 29. Candidates can challenge the answer key, but not their recorded responses.

Why now: about 22 lakh candidates will sit NEET on computers from 2027. The system they use must be ready before that.

The problem, broken into six failure types

Each failure type needs a different answer. One fix does not cover all of them.

1. Paper leak at the source

Seen in: NEET-UG 2026

Someone who sets or prints the paper shares it.

Saakshi: papers stay locked until two of three key holders release them. It cannot stop a setter who knows the questions.

2. Insider changes answers

Seen in: SSC Dhanbad, Bihar CHO (37 arrests)

A centre server or its operator edits or injects responses.

Saakshi: each answer is signed on the seat and chained. Any later edit is found and the true answer is recovered.

3. Outage and crashes

Seen in: CUET-UG 2026 (3,765 candidates re-tested), SSC 2025

Software or links fail during the exam.

Saakshi: the seat keeps answers on its own disk. Killed relays and cells were rebuilt with 0 answers lost.

4. Remote help and cheating tools

Seen in: SSC 2025 remote access, Bihar CHO proxy server

Remote-view software or hidden tools run on the exam PC.

Saakshi: a check before the exam blocks known remote tools by name. Flags go to a human, never an automatic penalty.

5. No proof for the candidate

Seen in: JEE Main 2025 (46 vs 29)

The candidate cannot check what was recorded.

Saakshi: each candidate gets a receipt slip. The public verify page compares it with the record.

6. Unfair handling after trouble

Seen in: NEET-UG 2024 (grace marks, 813 of 1,563 re-tested)

Who gets compensated, re-tested or re-conducted is argued in court.

Saakshi: a rule-based decision per candidate and per centre, shown with evidence. Tested on synthetic data only.

What the solution does well

It removes the need to trust any one insider. It keeps the exam running through failures. It gives the candidate proof.

Honest limits

A seat signature shows a record was not changed after the device made it. It does not show the candidate chose it. It cannot stop a paper setter from leaking questions. Analytics use synthetic data. Speed figures come from one machine. Tested on macOS and Windows.
